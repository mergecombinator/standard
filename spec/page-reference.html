The Merge Standard

The Merge Standard is a free, published set of agreements for forming ventures in national security. Three short documents carry a partnership from the first conversation to a company. We are the team you are missing, until we find the team you need. We do not bill for it. We get paid when you do.

Why it exists

Early paper is written for a relationship nobody has yet

Two people who want to build something together open a conversation about splits, scope, and exclusivity before either of them knows what the thing is. Counsel drafting for an undefined relationship writes for maximum protection. The paper comes back one-sided, both parties get defensive, and the work stops while the terms get argued.

The Merge Standard splits that negotiation into three stages and settles only what can honestly be settled at each one. Trust comes first, roles come second, equity comes last and only once.

How it works

A pursuit, start to finish

Here is what a twelve month cycle looks like when it works.

DAY 0
Both parties sign the Explore Agreement and each names one thing they will produce in thirty days. A prototype in front of five users. Ten customer conversations with notes. A read on whether the acquisition path exists.
DAY 1–30
The work gets done alongside everyone's real job. Both sides share freely under mutual confidentiality.
DAY 30
One review meeting. Each party puts their deliverable on the table. Go, stop, or one thirty-day extension.
MONTH 1–7
Pursue. Roles get assigned to named people with hours attached. Merge fills whichever seat is empty and starts recruiting its replacement. Milestones run against the formation triggers.
THE TRIGGER
A contract, grant, or award requires a legal entity to receive it, or a cofounder goes full time and needs equity. Money is what forces incorporation.
FORM
The company forms. Equity issues once, against defaults published the whole time. Merge hands its seat to the permanent holder.

The gate out of Explore

Three conditions, all required. Anything less is a stop or a single extension.

  • Every party produced what they named. Capacity claimed in a meeting and capacity demonstrated in thirty days are different numbers.
  • Demand exists outside the room. Five people who are not friends of the team described the problem in their own words, or there is a written expression of interest or money on the table.
  • Every core seat has a name against it. An unnamed technical founder is the most common reason a venture stalls here.

What does not require forming a company

Founders routinely incorporate months earlier than they need to. Merge's SAM registration and CAGE code can carry a federal pursuit through Explore and Pursue, and a fiscal sponsor can receive grant funding and tax-deductible donations. Neither stage requires a new entity. Forming becomes necessary when equity has to be issued, when an investor needs a clean cap table, or when an award lands that Merge should not hold.

Terms these documents do not contain

What Explore and Pursue leave out

Most of what makes early paper feel hostile is terms that belong at a later stage. Explore and Pursue contain none of the following.

  • Non-compete of any kind
  • Non-solicitation of employees or customers
  • Assignment or license of intellectual property
  • Work-for-hire language
  • Broad exclusivity. Pursue restricts one named opportunity, mutually, and nothing else
  • Fees, retainers, billed hours, or reimbursement
  • Any obligation to proceed to the next stage
  • A promise to negotiate future terms in good faith

That last omission is deliberate and legally load-bearing. See the section on teaming agreements.

Roles

Six seats. Pick one.

Problem Owner
The operator who confirms the gap is real. Non-equity by default.
Technical Founder
Owns the product and architecture. Builds the thing.
Venture Lead
Owns capital, contracts, hiring, and the P&L.
Studio
The vehicle, the relationships, the acquisition path, and whichever seat is empty.
Contributor
Scoped work with a defined end date.
Advisor
Named, a few hours a quarter, opens doors.

Merge fills an empty seat rather than selling a service. Any seat held on a part-time basis carries three things in writing: a term that ends at formation, a conversion to a position rather than a fee, and a succession obligation that makes recruiting the permanent holder a named deliverable. A seat missing any of the three is a retainer.

Comparison

How this relates to a teaming agreement

A teaming agreement under FAR Subpart 9.6 governs two existing companies bidding a single opportunity together. The Merge Standard governs whether a company should exist at all. The two operate at different stages, and a venture formed through Form will sign conventional teaming agreements with primes afterward like anyone else.

Conventional teaming agreementThe Merge Standard
Question it answersShould we bid this together?Should this company exist?
LifespanEnds with the bidEnds in a company or a clean stop
ExclusivityUsually broad, usually one-wayOne named opportunity, mutual, at Pursue only
Who drafts itWhichever party is largerPublished in advance, same for everyone
Future termsNegotiated laterPublished before you sign
Government disclosureTeam arrangement disclosed in the offerNo team arrangement formed, nothing to disclose yet

Conventional teaming agreements usually promise a future subcontract on terms to be negotiated later. Virginia courts, which govern a large share of government contracting relationships, have repeatedly held those to be unenforceable agreements to agree. The common fix is to front-load an entire subcontract into the teaming agreement, which few parties will do this early in a relationship.

The Merge Standard publishes the Form terms in advance instead. There is nothing left to negotiate at the end, so there is no agreement to agree, and you can read the formation terms before you sign the first page.

Y Combinator applied this approach to seed financing with the SAFE, and Common Paper has applied it to commercial contracts. The Merge Standard applies it to venture formation in national security.

FAQ

The documents

Can I use these without working with Merge?

Yes. Use them with anyone, for anything, including opportunities we will never touch and ventures that compete with ours. A document only one party can use is not a standard.

Can I change them?

You can do whatever you want with your own copy. We will not sign a modified version. If a term does not work, tell us and we will consider changing it in the next version for everyone.

My lawyer wants edits. What now?

Send us the edits. If they improve the document we will fold them into the next version and you get the improved one. If we disagree, we will say why. Either way the review is cheap, because the documents are one and two pages.

Why is there no equity until Form?

Equity assigned before roles are proven gets renegotiated later, and renegotiating equity ends partnerships. Roles get honest at Pursue because nobody has anything to lose yet by admitting what they can actually commit.

Why is there no non-compete?

A non-compete at the exploration stage restricts someone's livelihood in exchange for a conversation. If the venture is worth being in, people stay because of the equity, not the paper.

Is Pursue exclusive?

Narrowly. Neither party pursues that one named opportunity with a third party without telling the other first. Everything else stays open, including adjacent work. Either party can exit with fourteen days notice, which ends the restriction immediately.

What if we hit the triggers and someone does not want to form?

They say so and it ends. No penalty, no claim, no obligation to explain. Pursue states this in plain terms.

Is this a teaming agreement?

No. It governs whether a company should exist. A teaming agreement governs two existing companies bidding one opportunity. A venture formed this way will still sign conventional teaming agreements later.

Do I have to disclose this to the government?

Explore and Pursue do not create a contractor team arrangement, so FAR 9.603 disclosure does not apply to them. Once you actually team on an offer, normal disclosure rules apply.

Can a serving government employee sign one of these?

A Problem Owner can typically sign an Explore Agreement, which involves no money and no equity. Financial interests are a different question. A current federal employee, including a Guard or Reserve member in a duty status, generally cannot hold a financial interest in a company selling to their own organization under 18 U.S.C. § 208 and 5 CFR 2635. Problem Owner is a non-equity role by default, and anyone who thinks their situation differs should clear it with their ethics counselor before anything is issued.

What license are the documents under?

Creative Commons, free to use and adapt with attribution. The repository carries the exact terms.

How do versions work?

Every document is numbered and dated. Old versions stay published. If you signed 1.0, you can always read 1.0.

Working with Merge

What does Merge actually bring?

The contracting vehicle and CAGE code, program office and operator relationships, acquisition experience across OTA, SBIR, and FAR, back office, and a person in whichever seat is currently empty.

What do you charge?

Nothing. No fees, no retainers, no billed hours, at any stage. We take a position at formation and we get paid when the venture does.

What does Merge take at formation?

A position sized by the published default splits for the Studio role. The numbers are in the Form Agreement, which you can read before you sign Explore.

Will you take a seat in my company?

If a seat is empty and we can fill it, yes. Operations, product, or technology, depending on what is missing.

How long do you hold that seat?

Through Pursue. It ends at formation. Recruiting the permanent holder is a written deliverable owned by whoever is sitting in it, which is usually us.

Do I have to be in Hawaiʻi or the Indo-Pacific?

No. Our relationships and our attention are concentrated there, so that is where we are most useful.

Do I have to be a defense company?

No. We form ventures around operational problems for institutions that are failing the people who depend on them. Most of those are national security. Not all of them.

I already have a company. Does this apply?

Explore and Pursue work between two existing companies deciding whether to build something new together. Form covers the new entity. Your existing company is unaffected.
