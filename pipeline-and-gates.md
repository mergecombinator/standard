# Venture Pipeline and Gates

**Version 1.1**

The stages from an identified problem to an independent venture, and the
evidence required to pass each gate.

Each stage has defined work, a date, and a gate that requires evidence to pass.

## The six stages

| Stage | Duration | Vehicle | Equity | What it produces |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Signal** | Days | None | None | A named problem and a person who owns it |
| **1. Explore** | 30 days | None | None | One deliverable per party, and demand evidence |
| **2. Pursue** | 6 months | MC or fiscal sponsor | None | A working product and a funding or revenue event |
| **3. Form** | Weeks | New or existing entity | Yes, with vesting | An equity relationship recorded on a cap table |
| **4. Field** | 6 to 18 months | The venture | Vesting | Delivered contract or paying customers |
| **5. Scale** | Ongoing | The venture | Dilution | Permanent holders in every seat; the venture operates independently |

## Gate 0 to 1: Signal to Explore

A person identifies a problem they have experienced directly or observed in an
operator's work, and a second party is willing to spend thirty days evaluating
it. Explore carries no fees or obligations, so the threshold is deliberately
low.

A party unwilling to name a deliverable is not ready to sign an Explore
Agreement.

## Gate 1 to 2: Explore to Pursue

All three of the following are expected. Otherwise the parties conclude,
redirect, or extend once for thirty days.

1. **Every party completed its deliverable.** This is the most informative of
   the three, because it tests demonstrated capacity rather than stated
   capacity.
2. **Demand exists independently of the parties.** Five prospective customers or
   users not affiliated with either party described the problem, or a party
   holds a written expression of interest, a letter of intent, or a funding
   commitment.
3. **Every core role has a named holder.** An unfilled Technical Founder role is
   the most common reason a venture stalls at this gate.

## Gate 2 to 3: Pursue to Form

For an existing company bringing in a contributing partner, Form is signed when
the formation conditions in the parties' Pursue Agreement are met. A new company
is formed when one of the following requires an entity:

1. A contract, award, or grant is available and requires a legal entity to receive
   the funds.
2. Revenue requires its own bank account and tax ID.
3. Someone is going full time and needs equity to justify it.
4. An investor requires a clean cap table.

### What does not require forming early

| Need | Covered by | New entity? |
|---|---|---|
| Bid a federal contract | MC's SAM registration and CAGE 11TF7 | No |
| SBIR or STTR | MC as the small business awardee | No |
| Receive a foundation grant | Fiscal sponsor at ~10%, or MC if the grant permits for-profits | No |
| Take tax-deductible donations | Fiscal sponsor | No |
| Hire a contractor | MC | No |
| Sign an NDA or teaming agreement | MC | No |
| Issue equity to a cofounder | An entity | **Yes** |
| Take institutional investment | An entity | **Yes** |
| Separate the venture's liability from MC | An entity | **Yes** |

## Gate 3 to 4: Form to Field

Administrative rather than evidentiary: bank account open, EIN issued, cap table
current, Form Agreements signed, and intellectual property assigned to the
entity.

## Gate 4 to 5: Field to Scale

Both required.

1. **The thing works in the field.** A contract delivered and accepted, or paying
   customers at a level that covers direct costs.
2. **Every seat MC held on a part-time basis has a permanent holder.** The
   succession obligation in the Role Taxonomy is checked at this gate.

Merge Combinator's position then becomes a passive holding, and Merge Combinator
stops operating within the venture.

## Evidence-based gates

A gate based on a date passes when the date arrives, whether or not progress has
been made. A gate based on evidence passes only when something has been
produced. The dates in these documents schedule each review, and the evidence
decides it.
